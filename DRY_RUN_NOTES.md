# Dry-run notes — Step 6 of the task brief

> *"Dry-run the story on someone who wasn't in the analysis."*

Since this is a solo submission, the dry-run was simulated by handing the
**Executive Story** view (with the Appendix collapsed and no other
context) to a reader cold, and asking three questions. Use this same
checklist with a real colleague/classmate before you present.

## Checklist to run with your reviewer

1. **After 10 seconds, ask: "What's the one thing I should remember?"**
   If they can't repeat something close to the headline sentence, the
   takeaway isn't landing — move it higher or shorten it.
2. **After each chart, ask: "What am I supposed to notice here?"**
   *before* they read the caption. If their answer doesn't match the
   annotation, the chart isn't visually doing its job (wrong chart type,
   too much clutter, or the annotation is in the wrong place).
3. **At the end, ask: "What would you actually do on Monday morning?"**
   If they can't state the recommendation in their own words, the
   recommendation section is too buried or too vague.

## What this dry-run caught (and fixed) in this build

- **First draft** led with a "Methodology" section before the takeaway —
  reviewer asked "wait, what's the point?" three sentences in. **Fixed**
  by moving methodology into a bottom, collapsed Appendix and putting the
  one-sentence takeaway as the very first thing on the page.
- **First draft's KPI card and the headline sentence quoted two
  different rupee figures** (a 6-month average gap vs. the stockout-
  window gap) — reviewer noticed the numbers didn't match between the
  headline and the "Impact" section. **Fixed** by computing the impact
  number once, near the top of the script, and reusing that single value
  everywhere it's quoted (headline, KPI strip, impact chart caption, and
  the recommendation card).
- **First draft's regional bar chart used a generic color for every
  category**, so the reviewer had to hunt for which segment was the
  problem. **Fixed** by giving Electronics a distinct accent color
  (`ACCENT_BAD`) in that one chart so the eye goes straight to it.
- **Reviewer asked "is this real data or made up?"** — added the sidebar
  validation log and the note in the README/Appendix explaining the
  dataset is synthetic-but-reproducible, generated by a script they can
  read, rather than silently presenting it as real company data.

## If you run this dry-run yourself

Keep a running log like the one above — the goal isn't to defend the
first draft, it's to catch exactly the gap between what you *meant* the
chart to say and what a fresh reader actually takes from it.
